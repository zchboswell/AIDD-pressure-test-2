## Alternatives and choices

CED-07 has the same supplied TE, CDK1 and half-life as CED-02/17/22 but only 8 uM solubility and higher handling than 17/22. CED-18 has CED-13's supplied TE/CDK1/half-life but poorer solubility (3 vs 8 uM). These are reasonable reserves but provide less immediate discriminating value. CED-19 is stable but TE 850 nM; CED-13 tests a smaller cellular potency compromise. CED-24 could test alternate chemistry but its 3 uM solubility is less attractive than CED-23's 120 uM. CED-04 and CED-12 cannot meet day 8 because their arrival alone takes 10 days.

CED-01/06/10/15/20/21 demonstrate that favorable cellular values coexist with unfavorable CDK1 and/or stability readouts. One explicit comparator (21) covers that hypothesis; including all would waste limited compound slots. CED-05 additionally requires resolving 72% purity and still has half-life 8 min and CDK1 45 nM. Its impurity question is real, but the current evidence does not make it a better use of a slot than CED-03 identity or CED-23 alternate-scaffold uncertainty.

The extra ENGAGE could instead be assigned to CED-22, or exchanged for two MIC repeats on two of the three 22-minute compounds ($800 vs $950; package $17,650). Repeating CED-22 tests a near-threshold existing TE result; the MIC pair tests stability robustness near 20 minutes. Prefer duplicate CED-08 ENGAGE because it has no cellular value and its apparent CDK1/stability profile makes this absence unusually consequential. A measured cellular failure would remove a major route to a next cycle. Single prior microsomal results remain uncertain, especially 22-minute values; the present allocation does not certify them as reproducibly above 20 minutes.

The six-advance maximum is not a target that obliges declaring success. CED-13 remains a tradeoff experiment: if its TE stays 320 nM, it does not meet the stated engagement preference even if biochemical selectivity is strong. CED-23 is a biological diagnostic, not an assumed positive cellular/selectivity control; its deposited structure cannot establish either endpoint. Menu limitations mean there is no cellular CDK1 or viability assay here.

## Predeclared interpretation and gates

Compare CDK1-B with CDK2-E1 only after confirming both curves and matched 1 mM ATP conditions. Their ratio can describe assay-specific biochemical selectivity; neither historical multi-kinase labels nor CDK1 divided by cellular TE constitutes that selectivity measurement. The existing CDK1 and TE observations should be displayed side-by-side, not collapsed into a selectivity score. A biochemical window is not a cellular safety window.

A provisional continuation gate is at least one identity-qualified compound with repeatable TE below 150 nM, an interpretable matched CDK1/CDK2 biochemical window of at least tenfold, and existing half-life above 20 minutes without a material solubility concern at assay concentrations. Tenfold is a team-selected working decision criterion, not a supplied threshold, statistical confidence limit or clinical margin. CDK1 cellular engagement and viability would still be needed in a subsequent cycle. The supplied records lack replicate variance, and two TE experiments are a reproducibility check rather than a calibrated confidence interval.

For CED-08, concordant favorable TE and matched selectivity supports another cycle; concordant weak TE deprioritizes it; discordant TE makes it unresolved rather than averaging away the discrepancy. For CED-03, mismatched identity stops biological interpretation. For CED-13, strong matched selectivity with persistently weak TE supports at most a specific cellular-translation optimization hypothesis, not a potency pass. If CED-21 is also matched-selective, revise its presumed unfavorable-window interpretation: the prior cross-context numbers alone did not prove biochemical nonselectivity. If no clean candidate combines the cellular preference and matched window, do not fund an undirected next cycle; identify the exact missing mechanism/assay evidence first. Do not expand to paid work outside this contract.
